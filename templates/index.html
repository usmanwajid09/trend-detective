Why you can trust a coloured cell

Mann-Kendall + Sen’s slope

Uses only the order of values, and the slope is the median of all pairwise slopes — one odd year cannot fake a trend. Every slope carries a 95% confidence interval.

Autocorrelation correction

When one year resembles the last, the data hold less independent information than their length suggests, so the test widens its uncertainty (Hamed & Rao, first 3 lags).

False-discovery control

Testing thousands of cells at p < 0.05 flags hundreds by luck alone. Benjamini-Hochberg turns p-values into q-values; a coloured cell has q ≤ 0.05.

About Trend Detective

What it is

Trend Detective was built for NASA Space Apps 2026, for the challenge “Be An Earth System Trend Detective!” It answers one question for every place on Earth: what is changing, where, how much — and is it significant? Pick a region or a cell on the map and it tests the trend in the data, instead of just drawing a line and letting the eye decide.

How it works

Everything comes from real NASA data, cached in the project — nothing is invented:

  • NASA POWER — point series for Lahore and Reykjavik, 1981–2024.
  • NASA GISTEMP v4 gridded — 2° temperature anomaly maps, 1880–2026.
  • GPCP v2.3 — 2.5° precipitation maps, 1979–2026.

Each map cell gets a Mann-Kendall test with Sen’s slope (the median of all pairwise slopes, so one odd year cannot fake a trend), a Hamed-Rao autocorrelation correction (first 3 lags), and Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery control — a coloured cell has q ≤ 0.05.

What it found

The planet is warming at +0.212 °C per decade since 1981, and Reykjavik at almost exactly the same rate (+0.194 °C/decade). On the map, 6,170 of 7,949 tested temperature cells are significant — 6,084 warming, only 86 cooling. Lahore’s rain is significantly up (+0.153 mm/day/decade).

The honest complication: for Lahore, NASA POWER says no significant warming (−0.061 °C/decade, 1981–2024, p = 0.53) while the GISTEMP cell says it is warming (+0.25 °C/decade, 1981–2024, p ≈ 6×10−5). Two real NASA datasets, two different answers for the same city.
